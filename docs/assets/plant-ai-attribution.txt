EcoPlant IA — model attribution, fixed artifacts and limitations
==============================================================

EcoPlant integrates an existing open model. The model was not trained by
EcoPlant. The photo is processed locally by ONNX Runtime Web; the inference
module downloads public model files and does not upload the photo.

Preprocessing follows the author's browser demo: resize the shorter side to
256 pixels, center-crop 224×224, RGB float32 in NCHW order, divide by 255,
then normalize with means [0.485, 0.456, 0.406] and deviations
[0.229, 0.224, 0.225]. Input: pixel_values [1,3,224,224].
Outputs: logits [1,38], features [1,768]. Probabilities are computed by
stable softmax of the logits. OOD uses the maximum cosine similarity of
the 768-dimensional feature vector to the author's normalized centroids.

ECOPLANT INTEGRATION CHANGES
The binary model and centroids are not modified. EcoPlant adds Portuguese
presentation, verified downloads, bounded retries, cancellation, local
session caching, safe resource disposal, and neutral inconclusive reports.
An OOD result omits disease classes and confidence scores. EcoPlant also
abstains when the highest class probability is below 0.75, or predictions
are incomplete or inconsistent. plant-health.js shares this interpretation
across inference, reports, history and plant details. The defensive 0.15
top-two margin is already implied by normalized probabilities with a 0.75
leader. These thresholds are application policy, not scientifically
calibrated accuracy, sensitivity or field performance. Policy version:
ecoplant-health-v1. The interface distinguishes healthy appearance, possible
disease, possible pest, and inconclusive; it never reports a health percentage.

SCOPE AND LIMITATIONS
The model recognizes 38 crop–condition classes in 14 crops: apple,
blueberry, cherry, maize, grape, orange, peach, bell pepper, potato,
raspberry, soybean, squash, strawberry and tomato.
Blueberry, raspberry and soybean have only healthy training classes;
orange and squash have no healthy training class. Conclusions explicitly
note these gaps. A single leaf cannot establish the health of the whole plant.

The training data shows single detached leaves under controlled conditions.
Outdoor backgrounds, other species, several leaves, roots, stems, lighting,
nutrient problems and conditions absent from training can produce errors.
OOD rejection reduces forced guesses but does not detect every unsupported
image. A high softmax score is a preference among known classes, not a
probability that a disease is confirmed or that a whole plant is healthy.
Published test-set accuracy is not used as a per-photo confidence value.
The output is preliminary and does not prescribe pesticides or treatment.

VERIFICATION
The unchanged public INT8 model was loaded and executed using ONNX Runtime
Web 1.30.0 WASM on CPU. The production preprocessing and classification path
identified two official PlantVillage examples consistently with their folder
labels (Tomato___healthy and Apple___Apple_scab) and rejected an EcoPlant logo
as inconclusive. Uniform white and green inputs were also rejected by OOD.
These are functional checks, not an independent accuracy assessment: the
leaf examples may have been used in training. Unit tests use explicit mocks
and do not claim to validate the trained weights or recognition accuracy.
